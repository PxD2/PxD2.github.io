R1The film

A campaign you can watch

The Mud for Blood campaign is being made into a full feature, start to finish, before any trailer. These are written character scenes from the story, staged and shot for the screen. They are not recorded gameplay with a filter on top.

The game is the film set. The same streets, vehicles and soldiers the player fights through are the ones the camera moves through, so every scene matches the world you play in.

R2The story

The modern crusade

The United States has fallen. A few states hold. One company saw it coming and built what would be needed: PXD2, founded by the operator known as Nomad.

Out of it comes the PXD2 Peter and David Coalition Forces, fighting alongside a New Order alliance of American, Russian, British and Polish units. They wear one mark so they can tell each other apart on a crowded battlefield: a red Jerusalem cross, front and back.

Across the line stand the Socialists of America and their aligned bloc. The campaign is told from the Coalition side. In the MMO, both sides are playable.

We used to ride horse and wear steel. Now we ride steel horse and better steel.— Mud for Blood, campaign premise

R3The game

Built for top-end PCs

GLOBAL THEATERS: Mud for Blood is PXD2's first release and the first title in the franchise. It ships with two things: the story campaign and a two-sided FPS MMO. It plays on screen, or streamed from the PC to a VR headset, and it is VR treadmill capable.

Everything in the world can be destroyed, helicopters included. Vehicles take damage when they hit things. Aircraft that fly into walls do not fly out.

R4The floor

Made here, owned here

PXD2 Studio is a production studio for film, animation and games, run from a fully off-grid shop in Concho, Arizona. Solar runs the machines. The work stays on our own hardware.

We don't buy game assets. Every vehicle, weapon and building is modeled in-house in Blender and brought into Unreal, and each one carries a PXD2 name and a PXD2 livery.

  1. StoryScenes are written from the campaign, beat by beat.
  2. SetThe game map is dressed and lit as the location.
  3. CameraThe PXD2 rig flies the move in Blender.
  4. RenderTwo workstations share the queue, one job at a time.
  5. VoiceLines are recorded and matched to each character.
  6. ApprovalEvery shot is signed off by the founder before it goes in the cut.
