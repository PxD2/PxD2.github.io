Vee: Did they only fight? Please say no. I want the pegs and the dockets and the lantern to win.

Bell: No. And this is the episode that makes children love them forever, if you let it. Major John: magistrate, commons, Rural Hill. Adlai: clerk of Rowan thirty-seven years, UNC trustee. John Brevard: justice from Anson on, guardian with Alexander Osborne of the Davidson children. Joseph Graham: sheriff, senator, justice, general twice — not governor. William A. Graham: governor, senator, Navy. Thomas Polk: council of state, hosted Washington. William Polk: first president of the State Bank, UNC trustee forty-two years. James Knox Polk: the House, then the White House.

Lee: And a college. And a horse. And a coat that is still too big.

Bell: Davidson College, 1837, named for the uncle, land from the son at Beaver Dam, records in the archives there. UNC’s Colonial Records keep Graham’s own voice. Nassau Hall keeps the 1768 cousins in a book called Princetonians. You can love a person in a docket, in a wagon, in a lantern, in a notebook, in a horse coming home. That is how a child loves a house without becoming a courtier.

Vee: I love them. I love Mary and Violet and Hannah and Aunt Susie and the horse who had no lines.

Lee: I love them. My coat is still too big. That means I have growing to do. I will lend the horse. I will keep the docket. I will hang the coat where I can grow into it.

Bell: Grow. Copy one office. Recite who held it. The house runs from a county court to a White House without becoming a palace. That is the point. If you go to Davidson, ask the archives for Beaver Dam and the general. If you go to Chapel Hill, ask for Graham’s papers and the Colonial Records, volume twenty-two. If you go to Nassau, ask for Princetonians 1748 to 1768, Brevard and Osborne. None of those rooms will hug you. I will. The paper will wait. The horse already came home. You are the children who make the paper warm again.

Vee: We love them. We will copy. We will recite. Kitchen first. Pegs, then presidents.

Lee: Sheriff, not governor. One Davidson family. A president goes home. The law is king. Still a bell.

Bell: Then the house they made is still open. Sit at the table of offices one more minute and love them as jobs, not costumes. Magistrate. Clerk. Sheriff. Justice. Trustee. Senator. Governor. Navy. Speaker. President. Bank. College. Lantern. Horse. Bell. One intermarried house. Joseph Graham was not governor; William A. Graham was. The general is uncle to Major John and to Adlai — one Davidson family. Ephraim and Adlai, Princeton 1768. Thomas saved a bell. Mary was a hinge. Lincoln copied. Washington went home. Madison kept a notebook. Jackson stayed under the law. You copy one office and recite who held it, as if they could hear you, because that is how you bring them to life. Not with a cheer. With a name, a job, a kindness.

Vee: Aunt Susie, I hear you. Lantern. Cows. Home. Mary, I hear you. Kitchen. Pegs. Then a president if the pegs stay.

Lee: William Lee, I hear you. Fog. Horse. Hopewell. College. Adlai, I hear you. Thirty-seven years of ink. Joseph, I hear you. Sheriff, not governor.

Bell: If you go to Davidson, ask the archives for Beaver Dam and the general. If you go to Chapel Hill, ask for Graham’s papers and the Colonial Records, volume twenty-two. If you go to Nassau, ask for Princetonians 1748 to 1768, Brevard and Osborne. None of those rooms will hug you. I will. The paper will wait. The horse already came home. You are the children who make the paper warm again. I am going to ring three times, very softly, and you are going to sleep, and tomorrow you will remember a cousin, not a statue. That is a five-minute education. That is enough. Good night, hornets. Sleep like a nest. I will still be here when you copy in the morning.
