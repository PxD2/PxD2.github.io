Vee: The boy from Mary Wilson’s line sat in the White House. Start in the kitchen anyway. I will not skip Mary.

Bell: Bless you. James Knox Polk. Mecklenburg blood, Tennessee house, a Knox in the name for the captain’s people. Great-nephew of Colonel Thomas, who saved the bell. This is why we taught the daughters first. A republic is supposed to work like that. Not a palace. A family that learned to read and to muster. If you ever stand in Mecklenburg and say Polk, start with Mary and Thomas and William, then James. The White House is the last room, not the first.

Lee: Was he our cousin? In the wide cousin way?

Bell: Yes. Mary Wilson married Ezekiel, Thomas’s brother. The records run through the Polk papers and the Wilson marriages like a creek you can step in twice. Speaker of the United States House. Governor of Tennessee. Eleventh president. He came from the same web that kept Rural Hill and Hopewell. He worked. That is the living part. Not a cloud. A desk.

Vee: I will not be president. I will be Violet anyway. That feels braver today.

Bell: That is the bravest line in this episode. Copy: Mary Wilson. Recite: Knox in the name on purpose. Love a president who was a cousin from a river, not a prince from a cloud. The Polk stack in this house is militia, council, bank, university trustee, White House. Thomas saved a bell. William kept a bank and a college seat. James sat where laws are signed. None of that erases Violet’s kitchen.

Lee: Kitchen first. Then the House. Then, maybe, a president, if the pegs are still on the wall.

Vee: Mary Wilson first. Then James. I will draw a kitchen table longer than a White House.

Bell: You have the order. That is how a child loves a president without becoming a courtier. Picture Mary. Picture Ezekiel. Picture a baby given Knox because a captain had already been a neighbor and had fallen at Ramsour’s Mill. Picture Thomas wrapping a cousin-bell in straw. Picture James working late as Speaker. Start from the kitchen and you will not get lost in the marble.

Bell: Good house. Sit in Mary’s kitchen one more minute until the White House feels like a later room, not a cloud. Picture a creek. Picture Ezekiel. Picture a baby given Knox because Captain Patrick had already been a neighbor and had fallen at Ramsour’s Mill. Picture Thomas wrapping a cousin-bell in straw. Picture William with a hurt mouth still going on to a bank and a college seat for forty-two years. Picture James working late as Speaker, then Tennessee’s governor’s chair, then the eleventh presidency. None of that erases a peg on a kitchen wall. If the pegs are gone, the White House is only a cloud, and we do not love clouds in this show. A nest makes a president and still knows where the coats hang. Start from the kitchen. Recite Mary. Then you may say James without becoming a courtier.

Vee: Pegs, then presidents. I can keep that next to sheriff, then governor. Mary first. James after.

Lee: I will lend the horse even to a cousin who sat in a White House, as long as he still knows Mary. Copy: Mary Wilson married Ezekiel Polk. Recite: Knox is the middle name on purpose.

Bell: Davidson’s papers and the old county marriages will wait. I will wait on my beam. Ring once. Tomorrow Lincoln’s books — not our blood, our method. Few books, copied, recited. A fire instead of Nassau. You are already enrolled. Not certified. Enrolled. Bring a proposition you half hate. I will bring a hat to tip.
