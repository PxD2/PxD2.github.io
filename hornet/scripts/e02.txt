Bell: Samuel Wilson farmed on this river. His daughters’ marriages are a map. If you can love a map, you can love a country. Maps are not cold. A map is how a girl knows which ford her cousin will use, and which kitchen still has a peg for a coat.

Vee: I am named for Violet. Tell me about her like she is sitting here, not like a caption.

Bell: Violet Wilson married Major John Davidson of Rural Hill. He signed Mecklenburg’s paper. He kept a farm that was almost a fort of cousins. Their parlor was a council. If you had been a child there, you would have thought every adult was an uncle, because almost every adult was. Their girl Isabella later married Joseph Graham — the boy who would not stay down. So Graham is not a guest. He is Wilson and Davidson too.

Lee: And Mary? I like Mary because presidents hide in quiet names.

Bell: Mary Wilson married Ezekiel Polk. Their line runs to a boy named James Knox Polk. Knox is the middle name on purpose — for Captain Patrick Knox, whose daughter Hannah married Samuel Wilson’s son. So when a president carried Knox in his name, he carried a captain who had already been a neighbor. Knox is in the house. Reese is in the house. Caldwell is in the house. Sarah Davidson married Alexander Caldwell, son of Dr. David Caldwell, who kept a school. Teachers in the blood.

Vee: So if I say Wilson, I have already said Polk, Davidson, Graham, Knox, Caldwell.

Bell: You have said a militia. The records at Davidson and in the old county papers are full of these names stacked like plates. No one rode to war as a stranger. They rode to a cousin’s ford. The daughters did not marry famous posters. They married the boys next creek.

Lee: Did they like each other?

Bell: They argued like families. Then they mustered like families. That is the joke and the kindness. Violet’s people kept Rural Hill. Mary’s people kept a president. Hannah’s people kept a Knox in a White House name. You do not have to be a president to be the hinge. The daughters were the hinge.

Vee: I want to be a hinge. Hinges look small and then a whole door moves.

Lee: I want to lend a horse. And hang coats. Where did they put all the coats?

Bell: On a peg by the door, too big, like yours. Love a peg. Love a creek. Love a daughter who was a hinge. One more from the farm books: Samuel’s son married Hannah Knox after Captain Patrick Knox fell at Ramsour’s Mill. Grief in one kitchen became a middle name in another house years later. That is how a republic is supposed to work. Not a palace. A family that learned to read and to muster.

Vee: That sounds crowded and perfect. Did they all talk at once?

Bell: Of course. Then someone rang a bell, which is me, and they sat. Copy Mary, Violet, Hannah. Recite who they married. Ezekiel. Major John. Samuel’s son. The cartoon counted only if you can say the daughters first.

Lee: Mary married Ezekiel Polk. Violet married Major John. Hannah married Samuel’s son.

Vee: Isabella married Joseph Graham. I like that she married the boy who would not stay down.

Bell: Rebecca married Captain Alexander Brevard, brother of the doctor Ephraim. Sarah married a Caldwell. Betsy Lee married William Lee the second, the general’s own baby, at Beaver Dam. Violet’s girls braided the house until militia, court, college, and a presidency were one web. Start from the kitchen and you will not get lost in the marble.

Lee: Kitchen first. Then the river. Then the college.

Bell: Yes. If you ever stand in Mecklenburg and say Wilson, do not skip the girls. The records will wait at Davidson. Rural Hill’s parlor will wait in the story. I will wait on my beam. Copy tonight: Mary Wilson married Ezekiel Polk. Recite: Violet Wilson married Major John Davidson of Rural Hill. Then you have loved a map that is really a family.

Vee: I will draw the map with creeks, not borders.

Lee: I will put a peg on the map for coats.

Bell: Good cartographers. Ring once. Tomorrow the nest stings, and we will still begin with cousins. That is enough Wilson for one night. Sleep like a hinge that knows it is needed.
