Bell: Ring once, children. I hang in the pines of Hopewell, on the Catawba. I am a meeting-house bell. When I ring, a whole family of families comes walking: Wilson, Davidson, Polk, Brevard, Alexander, Reese, Knox, Caldwell, Graham. They married each other until the militia was almost one household. They did not arrive as posters. They arrived with muddy shoes and names you can copy.

Vee: Bell, are you the Liberty Bell? The one in the pictures? The crack and the big city?

Bell: Bless you. I am her cousin in this story. She rang for a country. I ring for a river and a nest of hornets. A Polk of this house was tasked to save her, so she would not be melted into a gun. We will get there. First you must love the people, or the offices will sound like they fell from the sky.

Lee: I want to love them. My coat is too big because it is a general’s coat. William Lee Davidson. People named a college after him. Is that showing off?

Bell: It is remembering. Davidson College sits on land his own baby boy later gave, at a house called Beaver Dam. The records live at Davidson, and at Chapel Hill, and in a brick hall far away called Nassau. We will walk those papers like a path in the woods. No running. No shouting at the river.

Vee: Start with a small thing. I like small things.

Bell: Small: Hopewell’s people buried their general at the church in a hurry, so the other army would not find him. They loved him that much. His name was William Lee. Lee wears his coat. Vee is named for Violet Wilson, who married into Rural Hill. You two are the children of this cartoon. I am the bell who will not let you forget they were cousins, not statues.

Lee: Cousins are better than statues. Statues don’t lend you a horse.

Vee: Or walk you to school. Or hang a coat on a peg.

Bell: Exactly. Five presidents will visit us — Washington, Madison, Jackson, Cousin Polk, Lincoln with his books. But they do not get the first chair. The first chair is a family that kept a court, a militia, a college, and a bell. Copy one name tonight. Recite it in the morning. Then you have watched this episode for real.

Lee: William Lee Davidson.

Vee: Violet Wilson.

Bell: And Hopewell’s Bell, who loves you. Ring twice, and sit. Before we go, I want you to see the church as a child would. Beatties Ford Road. Pines. A meeting house that is not a palace. Friends carried a general there in the dark. They did not wait for a sculptor. They waited for morning, and they kept the secret of where he lay so the other army could not boast.

Vee: I would have held the lantern and tried not to cry.

Lee: I would have held the horse and tried not to run.

Bell: Both are love. The college that wears his name still keeps letters and ledgers. Chapel Hill keeps Graham’s own handwriting in the Colonial Records. Nassau Hall keeps the 1768 cousins in a biographical book called Princetonians. None of that is a statue. It is paper you could hold. When I ring, I am calling you to paper, and to people who lent horses.

Vee: I will hold the paper carefully. I will not fold a corner.

Lee: I will not run with the horse in the hallway.

Bell: Then you already love them. Good. Episode one is a handshake. Imagine walking into Hopewell on a Sunday. You would not see generals first. You would see uncles. You would see daughters who were hinges. You would see a boy whose coat was too big, which is how every good office begins — too big, and then you grow.

Lee: When will the coat fit?

Bell: When your love is as wide as the sleeves. Not when you shout. Not when you pretend to be marble. When you can say a cousin’s name without making him a saint, and still want to lend him a horse. That is the whole show, hidden in the first ring.

Vee: I like that the presidents have to wait.

Bell: They wait because a republic is supposed to start in a meeting house, not in a cloud. Washington will go home. Madison will keep a notebook. Jackson will be loud under the law. Cousin Polk will come from Mary Wilson’s kitchen. Lincoln will copy by a fire. None of them get to skip the family. Copy tonight: Hopewell’s Bell rings this river. Recite in the morning: the Liberty Bell is her cousin, and a Polk of this house was tasked to save that cousin.

Lee: Hopewell’s Bell rings this river.

Vee: A Polk saved her cousin so she would still be a bell.

Bell: Good children. I am going to ring three times, very softly. Sit like a nest. Tomorrow we meet the daughters, who were the hinge. The rest is supper. You are already in the house.
