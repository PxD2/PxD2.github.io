Lee: Jackson is loud. Will he break my pencil? Will he break the yoke?

Bell: He is loud. A Carolina backcountry boy who said the people are the sovereign. He broke a bank and held a Union and used a hard hand. This show will not make him a stuffed angel. Love is not flattery. He is in this show because the backcountry thought a president could be a neighbor with a hard hand. Our people already knew neighbors with hard hands.

Vee: Can we still love a loud person? I like quiet lanterns.

Bell: You can love the office and tell the truth about the man. Lex rex, children. The law is king. Even when the people cheer. Even when the man is popular. Our civics hall already keeps Jacksonian lessons. Here we only say: a president is still under the law. If the people are sovereign, they are sovereign under law. That is Jacksonian and it is Hopewell too.

Lee: If I cheer too loud, will you ring at me?

Bell: I will ring at anyone who forgets the yoke. Jackson came from the same idea as this nest: the people, not a caucus of clerks. Keep that. Leave the rest to older rooms. Copy: the law is king. Recite it in a whisper, which is louder than a cheer.

Vee: Whisper: the law is king. Whisper: even if I want to cheer.

Lee: Whisper: the people, not a caucus of clerks. Whisper: still under the law.

Bell: Good. Loud love is fine. Loud lawlessness is not. Picture a meeting house that rings for both the people and the law. Picture a bank as a giant purse some neighbors feared. Picture the Union as a wagon that must not split. Picture a hard hand and do not clap yet. First ask: is the hand under the law? Then you have finished Jackson without becoming a poster.

Vee: Madison was a page. Jackson was a shout. Both sit in the house. The yoke holds both.

Bell: That is the sentence. One last kindness: Jackson is in the civics hall with longer lessons if you want them. This cartoon only asks you to keep the people and the law in the same sentence. Hopewell rang for both. So do we. We do not flatten him. We do not crown him.

Lee: I will not become a poster. Posters do not fit on pegs.

Vee: I will keep my whisper even when the room gets loud.

Bell: Then you have watched this episode for real. Sit with the loudness without covering your ears, and without clapping yet. The backcountry thought a president could be a neighbor. That was the kindness. The hard hand was the danger. A giant purse some neighbors feared. A wagon called Union that must not split. A meeting house that already knew the people are not a caucus of clerks. Lex rex anyway. The law is king even when the neighbor is popular. Madison was a page. Jackson was a shout. Both sit in the house. The yoke holds both. We do not flatten him. We do not crown him. Hopewell rang for both the people and the law.

Vee: I will keep my whisper even when the room gets loud. The people, and the law, in the same sentence.
