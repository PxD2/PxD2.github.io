Bell: Philadelphia, 1777. The British are coming. Bells make cannon if you melt them. The State House Bell — the one you call Liberty — had to leave town in a wagon, under straw. Colonel Thomas Polk of Charlotte, Fourth North Carolina Continental, was tasked. Two hundred men, hundreds of wagons, all the brass bells. I ring for a river. She rang for a country. A Polk of this house kept her a bell.

Lee: Did the wagon break? I hope somebody wrote that down, or I will think we made it up.

Bell: Somebody wrote it down. The Moravian diary at Bethlehem, the twenty-third and twenty-fourth of September, wrote: the wagon with the State House Bell broke in the street and had to be unloaded. The other bells went on. On the twenty-fourth, Polk arrived at Allentown. The bells were still bells. That is the record the state keeps, and Allentown still tells. Straw instead of glory.

Vee: I love a broken wagon that still saved a voice. Straw is a superpower.

Bell: His son William, later lieutenant colonel, was in that same North Carolina line. Germantown: a ball through the mouth while he shouted orders. Teeth gone, jaw hurt. Valley Forge to get well. Then Cowan’s Ford, Guilford, Eutaw. Last surviving field officer of the North Carolina Line when he died. He welcomed Lafayette years later to breakfast in Raleigh. The father hauled the cousin-bell. The son wore the later rank. One Polk house.

Lee: So our Bell and that Bell are kin. I will not melt a voice. I will wrap it.

Vee: I will wrap my voice in straw if anyone tries to make it a gun.

Bell: That is the speech. Copy: the twenty-fourth of September, 1777. Recite: still a bell. Love a colonel who chose straw instead of glory. Allentown hid the bells by Zion’s church, under the floor. When the British left Philadelphia, the bells came home still bells. A North Carolina colonel did that. Love him for straw, and for not melting, and for not abandoning a broken wagon in a street.

Lee: Straw is a superpower. A broken wagon is not the end. Unload. Repair. Go on.

Bell: Thomas had already called Mecklenburg’s meeting. He fought at Brandywine. He wintered at Valley Forge. Then the wagons. The Moravians watched the train pass, wrote the broken wagon into a diary, and went on with their work. That diary is why we do not have to guess. Picture two thousand pounds of bell, a cracked axle, men in a street who will not leave her. Picture floorboards. Picture me, her cousin, ringing for a river because she was still a bell.

Bell: Ring for the cousin in Philadelphia. Sit with the broken wagon one more minute until glory feels too shiny. Picture bells taken down so they would not be melted. Picture straw. Picture a street in Bethlehem and a Moravian with a diary who did not need to be a soldier to tell the truth. Picture two thousand pounds of State House Bell, an axle that failed, men who unloaded her instead of leaving her. Picture Allentown, Zion’s church, floorboards. Picture Thomas of Charlotte tasked with the train. Picture William’s hurt mouth in the same line. Picture me, her cousin on the Catawba, still ringing because she was still a bell.

Vee: Copy: 24 September 1777. Recite: still a bell. I will not skip the Moravians. Straw is a superpower.

Lee: I will not skip the straw. Glory can wait on a peg. Unload. Repair. Go on. A broken wagon is not the end.

Bell: Love a colonel who chose straw instead of glory. Ring for the wagon that broke and was not abandoned. Tomorrow the house they made — local, bench, militia, state, president — one intermarried family, and a college, and a docket, and a lantern, and you. Bring your growing. I will bring every office and I will not let Joseph be governor.
