Reform College at PXD2

A complete Bachelor of Arts. Four years, 120 credits. Built to compete with the education John Witherspoon gave at Princeton — languages, Euclid, moral philosophy, natural philosophy, Scripture, and the American founding — not to counterfeit the later research university.

What this is. A finished undergraduate curriculum, reading list, examination standard, and lecture map. The lectures already live in this hall (Civics, Resistance, STEM) and in free courses we cite: Hillsdale, MIT OpenCourseWare, Open Yale. Primary texts are public domain. A student who finishes the map, sits the examinations, and defends the thesis has done a BA’s work.

What this is not. A state-accredited diploma mill and not Princeton University. We do not sell a Princeton degree, use their seal, or pretend a bureau has blessed this. If PXD2 later charters a college, this catalog is the program. Until then it is an education, which is the thing Princeton was for when Witherspoon sat in Nassau Hall.

Why Princeton is the rival

From 1768 to 1794 Witherspoon’s College of New Jersey was not a graduate school with a souvenir undergraduate brand. It was a BA: Latin and Greek, mathematics, natural philosophy, and moral philosophy as the capstone — the same moral philosophy this hall already teaches from Knox, Rutherford, Craighead, and Witherspoon. That is the education we compete with.

The later university added departments, professional schools, and a different theory of man. Reform College does not chase that. It restores the BA as a single course of study in which a graduate can read a statute, a demonstration, a psalm, and a constitution without a translator from the current system.

“Civil liberty cannot be long preserved without virtue.”

John Witherspoon — the Princeton we are answering, not the one that replaced him.

Four-year map

Year I — grammar and demonstration

Fall: COL 101, LAT 101, LOG 101, MAT 101, THE 101, CIV 101. Spring: LAT 102, RHE 101, MAT 102, PHL 101, HIS 101. ~32 credits.

Year II — the laws of nature and of nations

Fall: GRE 201 (or HEB), PHY 101, PHL 201, CIV 201, WRT 201. Spring: GRE 202, CHM 101, HIS 201, concentration 1–2. ~32 credits.

Year III — office

PHL 301, CIV 301, PE 101, concentration 3–6. The resistance classes and the arms cases are this year, not a freshman slogan. ~30 credits.

Year IV — thesis

Concentration 7–9, THS 401 (research) and THS 402 (defense). The thesis is deposited as an HTML or PDF in HOLD under /public_html/college/theses/. Public. ~26 credits.

Examinations and thesis

  • Every course: one closed-book written examination on the primary text, and one demonstration (a Euclid proposition, a brief page, a translation, a problem set).
  • No course is passed by watching the films. The films are lecturers. The book is the course.
  • Thesis: 8,000–12,000 words, or a mathematical memoir of equivalent labor, defended in writing in the hall. Topic must sit in the concentration and cite at least one primary text from the core.
  • Honors: thesis distinguished by the examiners you name (two readers). We do not invent a Phi Beta Kappa chapter.

Until a charter exists, the “diploma” is the public thesis plus a signed transcript of examinations you actually sat. That is more honest than a purchased credential, and it is how a college begins.

Faculty of record

The faculty are the authors. Lecturers are cited, not employed as ornaments.

  • Moral philosophy: Aristotle, Rutherford, Knox, Witherspoon, Craighead — PxD2 resistance classes.
  • Founding: Madison, Hamilton, Jay, Brutus, Mason — two complete accounts.
  • Constitution: Hillsdale College free courses, enrolled by the student.
  • Mathematics: Euclid; Jerison (MIT 18.01); Strang (18.06); Hillsdale Euclid course.
  • Natural philosophy: Newton; Shankar (Yale); MIT 8.01 / 5.111 / 7.01.
  • Law: Blackstone; the cases on the resistance page; the coalitions brief as a worked example.

PxD2 hosts the college, the halls, and the FTP volume. It does not replace Euclid.
